# 2026-11-17

A baked world's terrain now has a texture. Until now it was six flat-coloured surfaces chosen per quad, so biome borders were staircases of cells and rivers
sawteeth of blue quads. `terrain::RenderTerrainTexture` paints one picture over the whole terrain by sampling height, slope, moisture and temperature
bilinearly in world space (smooth borders, grain on top, lake and sea beds dark, rivers painted in as blue ribbons); the bake writes it as
`<name>_terrain.png` and the terrain model has two primitives: the textured ground and flat water for lakes and the sea.

Getting it into the player found two gaps in the engine:
- There was no PNG writer outside the tools, and `stb_image_write` is implemented in several executables (a library implementing it again would clash), so
  `assets::EncodePngRgb8` writes a PNG with stored deflate blocks and CRC and Adler-32 (lossless, round-tripped through the engine's decoder in a test).
- The cook did not follow the external images of a `.glb`: `GltfFileReferences` returned nothing for it, so a textured `.glb` was packaged without its texture and
  the player drew white (a warning, "not in the package"). It reads a `.glb`'s JSON chunk alone now (not the megabytes of geometry, on every scan), and
  `RewriteGltfUris` repacks a `.glb` around its untouched binary chunk, so a content move keeps the model pointing at its texture too.

Smaller findings by looking at renders: the texture colours are display colours (encoding them as linear washed everything out under this sun), and thin river
water on steep carved ground is better painted into the texture than drawn as geometry.

The instancing question is answered in PERFORMANCE.md: not needed for bakes up to 5,000 objects (4.3 to 4.7 ms).

Not done: the lake shoreline is still a staircase (the water is quads); the terrain edge is an open lip; only a base colour (no normal map, no detail
textures); no mip-mapped texture splatting at close range; a 1024-pixel texture over 192 m is 5 texels a metre.
