# 2026-11-17

A baked world's terrain now has a texture. Until now it was six flat-coloured surfaces chosen per quad, so biome borders were staircases of cells and rivers
sawteeth of blue quads. `terrain::RenderTerrainTexture` paints one picture over the whole terrain by sampling height, slope, moisture and temperature
bilinearly in world space (smooth borders, grain on top, lake and sea beds dark, rivers painted in as blue ribbons); the bake writes it as
`<name>_terrain.png` and the terrain model has two primitives: the textured ground and flat water for lakes and the sea.

Getting it into the player found two gaps in the engine:
- There was no PNG writer outside the tools, and `stb_image_write` is implemented in several executables (a library implementing it again would clash), so
  `assets::EncodePngRgb8` writes a PNG with stored deflate blocks and CRC and Adler-32 (lossless, round-tripped through the engine's decoder in a test).
- The cook did not follow the external images of a `.glb`: `GltfFileReferences` returned nothing for it, so a textured `.glb` was packaged without its texture and
  the player drew white (a warning, "not in the package"). It reads a `.glb`'s JSON chunk alone now (not the megabytes of geometry, on every scan), and
  `RewriteGltfUris` repacks a `.glb` around its untouched binary chunk, so a content move keeps the model pointing at its texture too.

Smaller findings by looking at renders: the texture colours are display colours (encoding them as linear washed everything out under this sun), and thin river
water on steep carved ground is better painted into the texture than drawn as geometry.

The instancing question is answered in PERFORMANCE.md: not needed for bakes up to 5,000 objects (4.3 to 4.7 ms).

Not done: the lake shoreline is still a staircase (the water is quads); the terrain edge is an open lip; only a base colour (no normal map, no detail
textures); no mip-mapped texture splatting at close range; a 1024-pixel texture over 192 m is 5 texels a metre.

## The workspaces moved to the top, where the concept has them

The editor concept's second row is the workspace strip: World, Script, Blueprint, Animation, AI, Audio, UI, Material,
Sequence, Network. Ours had those as a wrapped grid of plain text buttons at the top of the tool list -- the one place
in the layout the design puts nothing.

They are now a full-width strip between the menu bar and the toolbar, each tab an icon and its name, the icon in its
category's colour, the active workspace a lit pill with an accent underline. The icons are drawn (globe, code page,
nodes, play head, chip, speaker, window, sphere, film strip, network nodes, bar chart), so no icon font or image file
enters the build. A tab opens its workspace's tool in the Editor window, the active tab is read from that tool, and the
strip reports its tab rectangles (`WorkspaceTabs`) the way the viewport reports its chips, so the headless test clicks
a tab for real instead of calling the API. The standalone hub window keeps the strip too; the tool list is now just
the search and the categories.

Test: `EditorWorkspace_WorkspaceStripOpensEachTool`.

## The level editor is one screen again: Hierarchy | Viewport | Inspector

The concept's fourth row is three panels side by side, the viewport in the middle. Ours was two: the Scene tool drew the
hierarchy and the Inspector, and the 3D view was a separate tool you had to open. So the tool the World tab opens showed
no world.

The Scene tool now takes a `SceneViewPanel*` (`SetSceneView`) and draws it between the two columns, taking the width they
leave (about a fifth for the hierarchy, a quarter for the Inspector, the picture the rest). The embedded view draws
without its own toolbar row -- in a column that narrow the row only clips -- so the gizmo's Move/Rotate/Scale choice
became another chip in the picture's top-left cluster, cycling on a click like the view-mode chip; W/E/R still switch it
from the keyboard. Without a view set, the panel keeps the two-pane layout it has on its own, which is what the isolated
scene tests draw.

Two defaults changed with it: the workspace strip's **World** tab opens the Scene tool rather than Terrain (Terrain is
still in the tool list), and a project with no remembered tool opens on Scene rather than on the first tool in the list,
which was an asset editor -- the first screen is now the scene, as in the concept. The shell's remembered tool still
wins, and `AETHER_EDITOR_TAB` still overrides both.

Tests: `ScenePanel_TheViewportSitsBetweenHierarchyAndInspector` (the panes do not overlap, the viewport is the widest,
the chips are inside it, and the two-pane fallback stays), the gizmo chip in `SceneView_ThePictureOwnsItsControls`, and
the World tab in `EditorWorkspace_WorkspaceStripOpensEachTool`.

## Play moves to the strip, and the toolbar ends in a breadcrumb

The concept's second row ends in a green **Play** chip, and its third row ends in `Game / Ironvale / Main World` with a
saved dot. Ours had Play buried mid-toolbar between the transform tools and Build, and nothing on the right.

Play now lives at the strip's right end: green while editing; while playing it is an amber **Pause** with **Step** and
**Stop** beside it; **Resume** when paused; the backend's name after a divider. The chips call the open document's own
`PlaySession` methods, so the strip, the menu, the toolbar and the status bar read the same state, and
`WorkspaceStripPlays` reports their rectangles so a test clicks them. The toolbar's own Play group is gone -- one set of
controls, where the concept draws it. A narrow window drops the right end instead of drawing it over the tabs.

The toolbar's right end is now the breadcrumb, built from the project file and the open document's display name, with a
dot that is green once the scene is saved and amber while it has changes, then "World saved" or "Unsaved changes". Save
is still the button; the breadcrumb only says where you are.

Tests: `EditorWorkspace_WorkspaceStripPlaysAndStopsTheScene` clicks Play, Pause and Stop at the recorded rectangles;
`EditorWorkspace_BreadcrumbNamesTheProjectAndTheOpenScene` checks the trail names the project file and the open document.

## The Inspector leads with who the entity is

The concept's Inspector column starts with a name in big type -- "BP_Player" -- and a muted line under it saying what
that is: "Blueprint instance · Gameplay". Ours started with a search box and a small `Name` row, so the panel never
said whose fields you were looking at.

The Inspector now draws that heading first. The title is the entity's name at 1.3x, with its frame only visible under
the pointer; it is the same rename field as before, so clicking it edits the name and typing still merges into one undo
step. The subtitle is the entity's kind from its components -- the word the hierarchy badge already uses -- reading
"Blueprint instance" when the entity belongs to a prefab instance, plus its first tag: "Blueprint instance · Gameplay",
"Model · Gameplay", "Camera". Several entities get "N entities" over "Multiple selection"; the shared Name box stays
below the search for them.

Tests: `ScenePanel_TheInspectorLeadsWithTheEntitysNameAndKind` draws the panel with one tagged entity, checks the title,
subtitle and stack order and that they sit inside the Inspector column, then adds a model and selects a second entity to
check the kind word and the multi-selection form.

## The menu bar gets the concept's mark and its search box

The concept's first row starts with an AETHER mark and ends with "Search commands, assets... Ctrl K". Ours started with
`Aether Editor - Windows - Vulkan - 57 tools` and had no search box; the palette could only be reached by Ctrl+Shift+P or
the View menu.

The shell now draws **AETHER** in the accent blue at the bar's left, then the menus, and the search box at its right
end (`EditorWorkspace::DrawCommandSearch`). The box and the palette's own field edit one string, so a search typed in one
shows in the other; a click in the box opens the palette, and closing the palette clears the text. Ctrl+K is a command
like the rest (`view.command_search`), so View lists it and the cheat sheet prints it.

Two traps, both found by the tests. The palette window used to take focus as it appeared, which is usually harmless --
except when it appears because the box was clicked: ImGui clears the active input when another window takes focus, so the
box lost the keyboard on the same frame and the first characters typed went nowhere. The palette now opens with
`ImGuiWindowFlags_NoFocusOnAppearing`. And `SetKeyboardFocusHere` cannot focus a widget inside the menu bar's menu layer,
so Ctrl+K puts the keyboard in the palette's field rather than the menu bar's box; both edit the same text, so which box
holds the cursor does not change what you see.

Tests: `EditorWorkspace_TheMenuBarSearchOpensThePalette` clicks the box at its recorded rectangle, types into it, and
checks the text is cleared on close; `EditorWorkspace_OpeningThePalettePutsTheKeyboardInTheSearchBox` opens the palette
and types, and `EditorWorkspace_GlobalChordsComeFromTheTable` now also presses Ctrl+K.

## The hierarchy header gets the concept's Hierarchy | Place tabs and its Place catalog

The concept's hierarchy column starts with "Hierarchy | Place" and ends with + and ... buttons; ours started straight
into the filter box. The panel now draws that header itself: two tab labels on invisible buttons with an accent
underline on the active one and `text.disabled` on the other, then + (the Create items as a popup) and ... (Paste,
Deselect All) at the right end. ImGui's own tab bar was not used because it takes the full column and reports no
rectangle per tab, and a test has to click them.

**Place** is the actor catalog: a "Search actors" box and one row per kind the Create menu offers, each with a
description. A row click calls the same `CreateEntity(kind)` the menu does, so the two cannot disagree about what a
Camera contains; the new entity is selected, named in the status line, and one undo removes it. `LastHierarchyHeader`
and `LastPlaceables` report the rectangles so `ScenePanel_TheHierarchyHeaderSwitchesToThePlaceCatalog` can click through
the page.

One trap, found by that test failing on its last assertion: adding the Camera sets the document's status line
("Added Camera"), which is drawn under the toolbar and pushes the whole hierarchy pane down by a text line. The
rectangles captured before the add no longer point at the tabs. The test now re-reads `LastHierarchyHeader()` after the
add instead of reusing the first frame's copy -- a reminder that a panel's geometry is a function of the document too,
not only of the layout.
