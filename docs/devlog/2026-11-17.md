# 2026-11-17

A baked world's terrain now has a texture. Until now it was six flat-coloured surfaces chosen per quad, so biome borders were staircases of cells and rivers
sawteeth of blue quads. `terrain::RenderTerrainTexture` paints one picture over the whole terrain by sampling height, slope, moisture and temperature
bilinearly in world space (smooth borders, grain on top, lake and sea beds dark, rivers painted in as blue ribbons); the bake writes it as
`<name>_terrain.png` and the terrain model has two primitives: the textured ground and flat water for lakes and the sea.

Getting it into the player found two gaps in the engine:
- There was no PNG writer outside the tools, and `stb_image_write` is implemented in several executables (a library implementing it again would clash), so
  `assets::EncodePngRgb8` writes a PNG with stored deflate blocks and CRC and Adler-32 (lossless, round-tripped through the engine's decoder in a test).
- The cook did not follow the external images of a `.glb`: `GltfFileReferences` returned nothing for it, so a textured `.glb` was packaged without its texture and
  the player drew white (a warning, "not in the package"). It reads a `.glb`'s JSON chunk alone now (not the megabytes of geometry, on every scan), and
  `RewriteGltfUris` repacks a `.glb` around its untouched binary chunk, so a content move keeps the model pointing at its texture too.

Smaller findings by looking at renders: the texture colours are display colours (encoding them as linear washed everything out under this sun), and thin river
water on steep carved ground is better painted into the texture than drawn as geometry.

The instancing question is answered in PERFORMANCE.md: not needed for bakes up to 5,000 objects (4.3 to 4.7 ms).

Not done: the lake shoreline is still a staircase (the water is quads); the terrain edge is an open lip; only a base colour (no normal map, no detail
textures); no mip-mapped texture splatting at close range; a 1024-pixel texture over 192 m is 5 texels a metre.

## The workspaces moved to the top, where the concept has them

The editor concept's second row is the workspace strip: World, Script, Blueprint, Animation, AI, Audio, UI, Material,
Sequence, Network. Ours had those as a wrapped grid of plain text buttons at the top of the tool list -- the one place
in the layout the design puts nothing.

They are now a full-width strip between the menu bar and the toolbar, each tab an icon and its name, the icon in its
category's colour, the active workspace a lit pill with an accent underline. The icons are drawn (globe, code page,
nodes, play head, chip, speaker, window, sphere, film strip, network nodes, bar chart), so no icon font or image file
enters the build. A tab opens its workspace's tool in the Editor window, the active tab is read from that tool, and the
strip reports its tab rectangles (`WorkspaceTabs`) the way the viewport reports its chips, so the headless test clicks
a tab for real instead of calling the API. The standalone hub window keeps the strip too; the tool list is now just
the search and the categories.

Test: `EditorWorkspace_WorkspaceStripOpensEachTool`.

## The level editor is one screen again: Hierarchy | Viewport | Inspector

The concept's fourth row is three panels side by side, the viewport in the middle. Ours was two: the Scene tool drew the
hierarchy and the Inspector, and the 3D view was a separate tool you had to open. So the tool the World tab opens showed
no world.

The Scene tool now takes a `SceneViewPanel*` (`SetSceneView`) and draws it between the two columns, taking the width they
leave (about a fifth for the hierarchy, a quarter for the Inspector, the picture the rest). The embedded view draws
without its own toolbar row -- in a column that narrow the row only clips -- so the gizmo's Move/Rotate/Scale choice
became another chip in the picture's top-left cluster, cycling on a click like the view-mode chip; W/E/R still switch it
from the keyboard. Without a view set, the panel keeps the two-pane layout it has on its own, which is what the isolated
scene tests draw.

Two defaults changed with it: the workspace strip's **World** tab opens the Scene tool rather than Terrain (Terrain is
still in the tool list), and a project with no remembered tool opens on Scene rather than on the first tool in the list,
which was an asset editor -- the first screen is now the scene, as in the concept. The shell's remembered tool still
wins, and `AETHER_EDITOR_TAB` still overrides both.

Tests: `ScenePanel_TheViewportSitsBetweenHierarchyAndInspector` (the panes do not overlap, the viewport is the widest,
the chips are inside it, and the two-pane fallback stays), the gizmo chip in `SceneView_ThePictureOwnsItsControls`, and
the World tab in `EditorWorkspace_WorkspaceStripOpensEachTool`.
