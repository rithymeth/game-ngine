# 2026-11-21

Animated water, with the scene shader's first time input.

- `FrameData` (the constants the scene shader reads once a frame) gains `time` (x: the game's time in seconds, wrapped hourly so a float keeps its precision for the
  scroll); 1216 to 1232 bytes, the HLSL struct kept in step, the layout assert in `test_light_clusters.cpp` updated.
- A material extension `AETHER_materials_water` (beside the existing `AETHER_materials_hair`): `GltfMaterial::water`, one bit in the draw's packed options (bit 11,
  next to double-sided), and in the scene shader a water material samples its normal map twice, scrolling the layers in different directions at different scales
  and averaging them, so the ripples slide through each other. Without the frame constants (a device without them) there is no time and the water stands still.
- The exporter writes the extension (`MaterialData::water`, with `extensionsUsed`), the baked water material sets it, and the ripple normal map is now a sum of waves
  with a whole number of crests across the map, so it tiles: a first version made from noise would have shown a seam running through the water as it scrolled.

Checked: a GPU test renders a flagged and an unflagged glossy quad at two game times three seconds apart (TAA off so there is no jitter): the same time twice is
identical (0.0000), the water moves (8.6 mean step of 255), the plain material does not (0.0000). A baked world rendered at two times differs only inside the lake
(4 percent of the pixels).

Not done: the colour texture does not scroll (the foam and the tint stay on the shore, which is what you want, but the water has no flow direction either); a
material's own speed and scale (they are constants in the shader, so every water material moves alike); the water's `MaterialData::water` is not serialised in the
Material sub-asset (the loader reads it from the file, which is what the player does); no terrain reflections; other effects that want time (swaying grass, flickering
lights) have the input now and have not been done.
