# 2026-11-20

Water that blends and ripples, with no change to the renderer. The renderer already draws glTF `BLEND` materials (a transparent pipeline) and reads normal-map textures, so
the "water shader" is a material:

- The water texture is RGBA now (`EncodePngRgba8`, a second PNG writer alongside the RGB one): its alpha is the opacity, 30 percent in the shallows (the sandy bed shows
  through) to 93 in the deep, with foam nearly solid (95). `EncodeGlb` writes a material's `alphaMode` (BLEND and MASK) and `normalTexture` (the images are listed once
  each however many slots name them), and the water's glTF material is a BLEND one with the texture.
- `terrain::RenderWaterNormalMap` makes the ripples: a tangent-space normal map over the same map, three scales of smooth noise (about 0.7, 2 and 6 m across), at the
  terrain texture's size so a ripple is several texels. The sun and the sky glint on it; the roughness is 0.1.

Checked in the player: the shallows are see-through (the bed shows, and through the water at the cut edge of the block), the foam stays solid, the ripples catch the light.

Not done: the water does not move. The scene shader has no time input and a glTF material has no way to scroll a texture (no `KHR_texture_transform` here, and one UV set
shared by colour and normal), so animated ripples want a time uniform in the shader, or a per-frame texture offset in the player: a renderer change I have not made.
No terrain reflections (the sky's only; planar or screen-space reflections are renderer work), and a blended surface does not cast a shadow or write depth, so
objects seen through it are sorted by the usual rules for transparency.
