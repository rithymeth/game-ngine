## Skeletal animation audit (A06), from the code

Checked the code, not the readiness matrix.

- **Exists and has tests:** glTF skin import, animation clips, graph, blend spaces, root motion, retargeting, IK
  (`test_animation*.cpp`, `test_anim_graph.cpp`, `test_ik.cpp`, `test_gltf_loader.cpp`). `test_aether01.cpp` checks the Kael
  rig's 13 skin matrices come from the animation graph. AETHER-01 ships rigged Kael and Warden glTFs.
- **Skinning is on the CPU.** `player/src/scene_renderer.cpp` (~line 1647-1701) blends every vertex against the joint
  matrices each frame and rewrites per-frame vertex buffers. There is no GPU skinning path (no joint palette in any
  shader). That cost scales with vertices times characters, which is the AAA crowd and boss-fight case.
- **Cook skips LODs and meshlets for skinned meshes** (`engine/src/cook/mesh_cook.cpp:155-176`), so skinned characters
  have no LOD chain.
- **Not found:** a test that checks the *rendered* deformation or the packaged-scene animation, which is what A06 asks for.

### Next, in order
1. Measure CPU skinning cost: frame time for N characters at the Kael/Warden vertex counts. No building before the number.
2. If it is a bottleneck, move skinning to the GPU (joint palette in the vertex stage) and add LODs for skinned meshes.
3. Add a rendered-deformation check to the proof run so A06 can be marked Proven.

### Measured (step 1 done)

CPU skinning is about 60 ns per vertex per frame, single-threaded. Kael (104 vertices) costs 0.007 ms: irrelevant. Ten
20k-vertex heroes cost 13.3 ms (80% of a 60 fps frame) and 100 NPCs of 5k vertices cost 29 ms (over budget). Full table in
`docs/design/PERFORMANCE.md`. Conclusion: GPU skinning is worth building for the AAA target, not for AETHER-01 itself.

### Faster CPU skinning (step 2 done)

Went to GPU skinning first, then looked at the RHI: graphics pipelines have no joint-palette binding and no skinned vertex
layout, so it is a D3D12 and Vulkan change, not a shader change. Did the cheap thing first, measured.

- Moved the player's inline loop into `assets::SkinVertices` (`engine/include/aether/assets/skinning.h`), so it is tested.
- **Rejected:** blending the joint matrices once per vertex. Not faster (10.7 ms vs 10.3 ms for ten heroes). Numbers kept in
  `PERFORMANCE.md`.
- **Kept:** `SkinVerticesParallel` over the job system, used by the player for meshes of 4,096+ vertices. Bit-identical to
  serial. Ten 20k-vertex heroes: 10.4 ms to 2.76 ms (3.8x). A 100 x 5k crowd: 25.1 ms to 9.7 ms (2.6x).
- Tests: `Skinning_MatchesOracle`, `Skinning_EdgeCasesMatchOracle`, `Skinning_ParallelMatchesSerial`.

Still open: per-character parallelism for crowds, GPU skinning (when a scene needs it), skinned LODs, and the
rendered-deformation check that A06 still needs.

## Cooking at scale (priority 4), 10 November 2026

Measured before building: a generated project of 2,000 textures and 1,000 models, cooked three times.

- **Contradicted an assumption:** "the cook is incremental" was true for textures (warm cook 0.24 s against 4.3 s cold) and false
  for models (6.3 s warm against 8.2 s cold). Every import was a cache hit; the time was `CookMesh` rebuilding LODs and meshlets
  for every mesh on every cook, because cooked meshes never went through the derived data cache.
- **Done:** cooked meshes are cached (`cooker.cpp`, key: imported mesh bytes, `MeshCookSettings`, platform). Warm cook of 1,000
  models 6.3 s to 3.9 s; mixed project 6.8 s to 4.3 s. Table in `docs/design/PERFORMANCE.md`.
- **Test:** `Cook_AtScaleIsIncrementalAndDeterministic` (450 assets, about 3 s): byte-identical repeat, exactly two assets redone
  after two sources change, GUIDs stable, cached output equals uncached output, damaged cache entries rebuilt. Checked by removing
  the settings from the cache key: the test fails there.
- **Still open:** the remaining warm time is the archive (single-threaded compression is about half of it, and the whole archive is
  rewritten whatever changed). Parallel compression or reusing the previous archive's entries comes next, measured first.

### Archive packing on several threads (same day)

Did the step the entry above left open. `PakWriter::Build` now checksums and compresses entries on the machine's threads and lays
them out in order, so the bytes do not depend on the count (`Pak_ThreadCountDoesNotChangeTheBytes`). Warm cook of 1,000 models
3.9 s to 1.65 s on 4 cores; cold 7.3 to 9.2 s down to 4.7 s. Next in line, measured first: importing and cooking assets in
parallel (the cold cook), and not rewriting unchanged entries.

### Texture block compression on several threads (same day)

The scale project's 16x16 textures hid the real cold-cook cost, which is block compression of large textures (BC7 2048x2048:
6.0 s on one thread). `CookTexture` now encodes rows of blocks on the machine's threads: 3.4 to 3.6x on 4 cores for BC7, BC3 and
BC1, 1.7x for BC5, bytes identical at every thread count (`TextureCook_ThreadCountDoesNotChangeTheBytes`). Open: ASTC, mip
generation, and cooking several assets at once.

## Large scenes (priority 2): subtree walks were quadratic, 10 November 2026

Measured a 5,000 to 50,000 entity tree before touching anything. World transforms are linear. `Lifecycle::Destroy` and
`MakePrefab` were not: each called `ChildrenOf`, a scan of the whole world, once per entity of the subtree (50,000 entities:
1.7 s to destroy the root). A one-pass `ChildIndex` makes destroy linear (11.6 ms) and leaves `MakePrefab` with its JSON work
(153 ms). Tests: the index against `ChildrenOf` on a 600-entity forest, subtree order, a parent cycle, and a 4,000-entity destroy
that takes exactly its subtree, children first. Table in `docs/design/PERFORMANCE.md`.
