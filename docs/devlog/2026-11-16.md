# 2026-11-16

Four plant generators in `engine/include/aether/assets/procedural_mesh.h`: `GenerateShrub` (leaf masses on thin stems), `GenerateGrass` (two-sided tapering
ribbons, flat shaded), `GenerateReeds` (stalks with cattail heads) and `GenerateCactus` (a column with a rounded top and up to four arms), as kinds Shrub, Grass,
Reeds and Cactus for `GenerateVariant`. The Procedural World editor's starter rules now all have a mesh family (the user's own added rules still start with none),
so the shrub, grass, reed and cactus rules bake instead of baking as nothing.

A bake's object budget used to be spread evenly over every instance, which with a grass rule of 40 tufts per 100 square metres would have thinned the trees by
the same factor as the grass. It follows the scatter's pecking order now: earlier rules (trees) keep every instance while there is room, and the grass is
thinned to what is left. A test holds it (all pines survive a budget that grass would have eaten).

Checked in the player: a gallery of every plant up close (shrubs, grass tufts, reeds and cacti read correctly), and a re-baked Temperate world with 3,000 objects.
The tufts are about 0.5 m, so they only show up close; that is the scale of grass.

Not done: a flower or bush variant with colour, grass that sways (no wind in the shader), LODs or impostors for the 3,000-object scenes (one draw call each),
and a grass card with a texture (the generator has no textures).

## Copying a whole component (parity stage 4)

The Inspector could already copy, paste and reset one property; the component card itself had only "Remove Component"
on a right-click nobody would guess. Every component header now has a «...» button at its right edge (right-clicking the
header opens the same menu) with Copy Component, Paste Component Values, Reset Component, Revert Component to Prefab and
Remove Component, acting on one entity or on the whole selection.

The component clipboard is its own, apart from the single-value one, typed by the reflected type and owning its copy,
so Paste is offered only on a component of the copied type and later edits at the source do not leak into it. Paste and
Reset write every field the Inspector shows — read-only fields included, because a read-only field is kept in step
with an editable one (a model's path with its asset) and pasting one without the other would split them — and never a
hidden field, which is runtime state. Each field that differs anywhere becomes one `SetFieldOnEntitiesCommand` carrying
every entity's own old value, grouped in one transaction: one undo step puts each entity back the way it was, and a
paste into a prefab instance records an override for each field it actually changed, as typing would have. A selection
where one entity lacks the component is refused whole rather than half applied, and an action that changes nothing
records nothing.

Tests: `tests/test_component_card.cpp` (5). Not done: reordering cards (the ECS keeps no per-entity component order to
move a card within) and the multi-selection screenshot gate.

## Dragging several entities in the hierarchy (parity stage 4)

The hierarchy could select several entities, then delete, duplicate or rename them together, but a drag still carried
only the row under the mouse. Worse, it could not carry more: pressing a selected row collapsed the selection to that
row on the press, before any drag could start. Now a press on a selected row of a multi-selection waits until release.
If it was a click, the selection becomes that row as before. If it became a drag, the selection stays and travels.

The entity payload is now one or more `Entity` values back to back. A drop on a row or on the heading reparents all of
them in one transaction ("Parent 3 entities", "Move 3 entities to the root"). An entity whose ancestor is also carried
is left under that ancestor, so dragging a parent together with its children moves the subtree intact instead of
flattening it under the target. An entity that would go under its own descendant is skipped, and the status line says
how many were skipped. The others still land rather than the whole drop being refused. "Move to the scene root" in the
row menu acts on the selection the same way Delete does.

Tests: `tests/test_hierarchy_drag.cpp` (6), two of them real ImGui drags: one from a selected row, and one click that
must still collapse the selection. Not done: an above/below drop indicator. Siblings are ordered by name, so there is no
order for a drop to choose.

## Every override of an instance in one list (parity stage 4)

A prefab instance's overrides could already be seen one field at a time, as blue labels on the inspected entity's cards
with a Revert on each. Nothing showed what an instance changed as a whole. An override on a child entity was invisible
until that child was selected, and an orphaned override could not be seen at all. An orphaned override is one whose
field, component or entity the prefab no longer has. It is kept so no data is lost, but it applies to nothing.

The Inspector now has "Overrides in this instance (N)" on every entity of an instance. The rows cover all the entities
of the instance. Each row names the entity and `Component.field_path` and shows the prefab's value beside the
instance's, read from the flattened prefab at the same path. Each row has a Revert of its own, and the list has Revert
Whole Instance; each is one undo step.

Reverting an orphaned row exposed a gap in `RevertPrefabOverrideCommand`. It named the component by `ComponentId`, so
an override for a type that is no longer registered could only be reached as "every component of the entity", which
also reverts that entity's live overrides. `RevertPrefabOverrideCommand::ByName` reverts by the declared name the
override itself carries. The test reverts one such row and checks that the entity's other overrides survive.

Tests: `tests/test_instance_overrides.cpp` (5). Not done: Apply to Prefab from the Inspector (saving a prefab asset
goes through the MCP's asset host today) and restoring removed entities.

## What each hierarchy row is (parity stage 4)

Every row of the hierarchy was a bare name. Telling a light from a camera from an empty group meant selecting each one,
and a prefab instance looked like any other entity.

Rows now start with a small coloured badge with a letter, such as L for a light, C for a camera, M for a model, P for
physics and G for an empty group. The kind is judged by component type names (`ComponentKind`) rather than by
including each module's headers. So the hierarchy keeps no list of modules, and a kit's components get a badge when
their names say what they are. The rules are ordered. Physics is checked before models, so `MeshCollider` is a collider
rather than a mesh, and a camera with a model on it reads as a camera.

Prefab instances are named in the same blue the Inspector uses for overrides, or in red when the instance's prefab
can't be found. An entity with overrides of its own gets the Inspector's override bar at the row's edge, which is the
cue to open the instance override list that landed this morning.

The badge and the name are drawn over the tree node rather than as widgets of their own. A second item on the row would
have split selection, dragging and the context menu between them. The multi-drag tests from earlier today, which drive
real rows, still pass unchanged.

Tests: `tests/test_hierarchy_rows.cpp` (4). The badges are letters because the editor has no icon font. Eye and lock
toggles are next and need editor-only visibility and pick state.

## Hiding and locking a row (parity stage 4)

The hierarchy could already filter, badge and drag rows, but nothing on it changed what the viewport would touch. The
eye and the lock now end every row, drawn as shapes rather than items so pressing them acts on the toggle instead of
the selection.

Hiding is not new state: the engine already has `Active`, the scene already saves it, and the render scene, picking and
update already honour it through `IsActiveInHierarchy`. The eye goes through it, so hiding a parent hides its children
everywhere with no extra wiring. `SetEntityActiveCommand` records what it found, including "no `Active` component at
all", so undo of a first hide leaves the entity exactly as `CreateEntity` made it. Hiding a selection is one transaction.

Locking has no engine meaning, so it lives in the document as an editor-only set of GUIDs. The viewport's pick, stack,
marquee and gizmo subject checks all ask `IsLocked`; the hierarchy still selects, renames, reparents and inspects a
locked entity, which is the point of a lock. It is not saved and records no undo, and a new scene starts unlocked.

Both are also on the row menu for the whole selection. Tests: `tests/test_hierarchy_visibility.cpp` (5), including the
render scene losing a hidden entity and a parent-hidden child.

## The editor said nothing about its tools, and the viewport drew one kind of box

Opening the editor showed a list of tool names with no hint of what any of them did, a Terrain window pinned to the
right whatever was selected, and a 3D view in which every entity -- camera, light, player -- was the same grey box.

The tool list now says what a tool is for. Every registration carries one sentence (`ToolDescription`); the list draws
the category as a coloured badge before the name and the sentence underneath, and the sentence is also the row's
tooltip. The open tool repeats its name, category and description above its contents, and the search matches the
description too. `EditorWorkspace::DrawToolList()` and `DrawSelectedTool()` are the hub split in two, so a host can dock
them apart; `DrawHub()` composes them side by side as before.

The shell docks the split: Tools is a 300 px column on the left, Editor fills the middle, and Debug is the only window
a preset moves (bottom strip by default). The Terrain window the old layout always docked on the right is gone; a tool
gets its own window only when Pop out asks for one. Layouts saved before this change name windows that no longer exist,
so the panel ini is renamed to `editor_panels.ini.old` once and the default arrangement rebuilt, and a preset of that
vintage is ignored instead of leaving the new windows floating.

In the viewport, colour comes from the entity's kind (`EntityKindColor`, shared with the hierarchy badge), every kind
except a model gets an icon at its origin (camera body and lens, sun, speaker, page, sparks), markers are named whether
or not they are selected, and a legend lists the kinds in the scene -- all under Helpers. The default scene was part of
the problem: "Directional Light" had no light and "Player" had no script, so it really was five empty boxes. NewScene
now adds a rotated `DirectionalLight` and a `ScriptComponent` on the player.

Tests: `EditorWorkspace_HasEveryTool` (every tool has a real description), `EditorWorkspace_ToolListAndOpenToolDrawApart`
(the two panels draw headless), and `SceneView_EntityKindsAreColouredIconedAndNamed` (the kinds classify, their colours
differ, and the icons, names and legend draw more vertices than a scene of plain entities).

## The viewport had no chrome, and a camera was still just a box

The design for this editor is `docs/design/qt-editor-concept.png`, and its viewport wears its controls inside the
picture: the viewport's name, its projection, its look and Show along the top left; the gizmo's space, the snap step and
the grid along the top right; the selection along the bottom left and the scene's counts along the bottom right. Ours had
a text row above the picture and nothing in it.

Those controls are now chips drawn into the picture and hit-tested by the panel before anything else sees the mouse:
the projection flips, the mode cycles Wireframe, Lit, Unlit, Normals, the grid and snap and space toggle, and Show opens
the same checkboxes as before. A click on a chip neither selects nor moves the entity behind it, and the hover pick and
the tools' input are suppressed while the mouse is over one. The chips remember their rectangles
(`SceneViewPanel::LastChrome()`), so the test clicks them at their real positions rather than at guessed pixels, and then
still selects an entity by clicking the picture itself.

Camera and light entities now draw what they do, not just where they are: a camera's frustum (four rays and the far
rectangle its FOV or orthographic height gives it, with an up tick), a point light's three rings at its range, a spot
light's disc and spokes, and a directional light's arrow and sun ring. The values are read from the components through
their reflected fields, so the portable editor stays free of the renderer. The legend moved down under the new top-right
chips.

Tests: `SceneView_ThePictureOwnsItsControls` and `SceneView_CamerasAndLightsDrawWhatTheyDo`.
