# Making a 2D game

The 2D toolkit lives in the `sprite2d` module. Start from the **2D Platformer**
template and read it.

## Sprites

A **sprite atlas** (`.aatlas`) is one texture with named frames (a pixel
rectangle and a pivot) and named animation clips. Cut a sprite sheet into a
grid, or pack separate images into one texture. A `Sprite` component draws a
frame of an atlas at its entity (x and y position, z depth, rotation about z);
a `SpriteAnimator` plays one of the atlas's clips. Sprites draw back to front by
sorting layer, then order, then z.

## Tilemaps

A **tileset** (`.atileset`) cuts a texture into tiles, marks which are solid,
and holds **autotiles**: sixteen tile choices indexed by which of a cell's four
neighbours match, so painting a block of ground picks the edges and corners for
you. A **tilemap** (`.atilemap`) is layers of cells over a tileset; a
`TilemapRenderer` draws it. In the editor, **Tools > 2D > Tilemap** paints,
fills and erases, edits layers, sets solid tiles and rules, and undoes strokes.

## 2D physics

Give an entity a `Rigidbody2D` (static, kinematic or dynamic) and a `Collider2D`
(a box or a circle; friction, restitution, a trigger flag, a layer and a mask).
Solid tiles of every tilemap collide too. Bodies don't rotate, which is what
platformers and top-down games want. Ask `Physics2D` for contact and trigger
events, `IsGrounded`, `WallSide`, `Raycast` and `OverlapBox`.

## The platformer character

`PlatformerController2D` on a body turns the Move and Jump actions into running
and jumping with coyote time, jump buffering, variable jump height and a faster
fall; every number is a setting in the Inspector. `CameraFollow2D` makes a
camera follow it, optionally inside bounds.

## Lights

`Light2D` is a global, point or spot light with a colour, intensity, radius and
falloff; `ShadowCaster2D` boxes and solid tiles cast shadows. The module
computes the light at a point, a light map over a region, and each light's
visibility polygon, ready for a renderer to draw.

## The pixel-perfect camera

Set an orthographic camera 11.25 units high at 16 pixels a unit for a 320 x 180
view. `PixelPerfectCamera` shows a reference resolution at the largest
whole-number scale the window allows, with bars around it, and snaps positions
to the art's pixel grid.
